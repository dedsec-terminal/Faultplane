Here is a summary of the article in 3 concise paragraphs:

A coordinated cyberattack targeted over 30 community water systems in Minnesota on July 26 and 27, prompting a statewide cybersecurity response. The attack affected operational technology, causing disruptions to water treatment and distribution systems. Several cities, including Braham, Plymouth, South St. Paul, and Maple Plain, reported outages, communications failures, or affected automated controls, with Braham's water plant going offline and residents being asked to minimize water use.

The Minnesota IT Services (MNIT) agency confirmed that the incidents shared common characteristics, including timing, methods of access, and targeted infrastructure, indicating a coordinated attack. However, investigators have not yet determined whether a single actor was responsible for all the incidents. MNIT is working with state agencies, federal partners, and affected utilities to contain, investigate, and recover from the attack. The agency has not publicly identified the attacker, affected products, or exploited vulnerability, and it is unclear whether data was stolen.

The attack has raised concerns about the vulnerability of critical infrastructure to cyber threats. While there is no official attribution, the tactics used in the attack are consistent with those attributed to CyberAv3ngers, a group affiliated with Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has issued guidance on defensive measures, including logging cellular modem connections, restricting controller access, and inspecting project files for unauthorized changes. The investigation is ongoing, and responders are continuing to assess affected systems to prevent further disruptions to critical services.

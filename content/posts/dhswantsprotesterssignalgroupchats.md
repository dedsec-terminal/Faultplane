The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) is seeking access to private Signal group chats used by protesters in Maine to coordinate responses to immigration enforcement activity. The request is part of a lawsuit, Hilton v. Noem, in which plaintiffs accuse DHS of violating their First Amendment rights by surveilling their activities. The government's request includes a list of protests attended by the plaintiffs, as well as any communications reflecting their beliefs and opinions about law enforcement.

The plaintiffs' attorneys have refused to turn over the community group Signal chats, arguing that doing so would be a First Amendment violation. They have turned over smaller Signal group chats, but with redactions to protect the contact information of non-plaintiffs and communications that could reveal the group's priorities or tactics. The government has also deposed plaintiffs and asked questions about the community Signal group chats, including whether public officials or outside groups are participating.

The plaintiffs' attorneys and civil liberties groups are pushing back against the government's request, arguing that it is an attempt to intrude on the fundamental right to engage in collective action. They argue that individuals should not have to give up their right to expressive association in order to sue to protect their First Amendment rights. The case highlights the ongoing tension between the government's efforts to monitor and suppress criticism of its immigration policies and the rights of individuals to protest and organize.

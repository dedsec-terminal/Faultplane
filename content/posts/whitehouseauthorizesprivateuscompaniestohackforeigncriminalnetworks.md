The White House has authorized private US companies to conduct offensive cyber operations against foreign criminal networks under the control and oversight of the US government. This move aims to utilize the innovative capabilities of the private sector to combat cybercrime, particularly transnational criminal organizations (TCOs) that run ransomware, phishing, and sextortion campaigns against Americans from abroad. The decision builds on an earlier executive order addressing cybercrime and fraud targeting American citizens, with American consumers reported to have lost over $20.8 billion to cyber-enabled crime in 2025.

The program will be run by the Homeland Security Task Force's National Coordination Center, led by two Executive Directors from the Department of Justice and the Department of Homeland Security. Participating companies will be required to form agreements with other private entities and federal, state, local, tribal, and territorial agencies to gather intelligence on criminal networks and propose operations against them. The program outlines two types of operations: Cyber Surveillance Operations, which involve accessing a target's systems without authorization to collect information, and Cyber Effects Operations, which involve manipulating or disrupting information systems.

To ensure accountability and compliance, companies participating in the program must maintain a bond or escrow of at least $1 million and face annual review. They must also cease operations and notify the National Coordination Center if they unintentionally target a US person or system. Any activity directed at a US person requires prior authorization, and the program will be implemented consistent with applicable law and subject to the availability of appropriations. The White House believes that leveraging the private sector's capabilities will provide a critical offensive cyber advantage for the United States in combating cybercrime.

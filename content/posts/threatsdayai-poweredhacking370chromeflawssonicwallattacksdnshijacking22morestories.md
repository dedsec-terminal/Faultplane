This week's ThreatsDay Bulletin highlights various cyber threats, including phishing attacks, custom ransomware, and malware campaigns. A cybercrime group known as xplogs22 has been targeting Russia and other CIS countries with phishing emails that deliver XWorm malware. Additionally, a financially motivated extortion group called Toy Ghouls has been targeting organizations in Russia with a custom ransomware family called GenieLocker. Other threats include the CastleLoader malware loader, which has been used to distribute payloads tied to the Needle Stealer framework, and a fileless WebDAV execution technique that tricks victims into pasting a command into the Windows Run dialog.

Other notable threats include a fake Claude guide that spreads MacSync Stealer malware, a Russian-speaking threat group behind an active campaign called Operation STANDOFF that combines commodity-malware distribution and influence operations, and a vulnerability in the My Eicher fleet management system that enabled account takeover. A Chinese-speaking threat actor has also been carrying out an AI-enabled autonomous hacking campaign, targeting infrastructure using seven vulnerabilities and leveraging DeepSeek and Hermes Agent to orchestrate the operation. Furthermore, a covert Linux XMRig campaign has been exploiting trusted access and weaponizing Pluggable Authentication Modules (PAM) to deploy a highly customized XMRig botnet implant.

The article also mentions other threats, including a fake recruiter delivering a stealer malware, a security incident at Australian energy company Origin Energy that affected 900,000 customers, and a threat to healthcare SaaS accounts. The threats are constantly evolving, and it is essential to stay informed and take necessary precautions to protect against them. The article concludes by emphasizing the importance of being aware of these threats and taking steps to prevent them, such as being cautious when clicking on links or installing software, and keeping software and systems up to date with the latest security patches.

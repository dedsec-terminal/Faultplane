Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article "Growing Up The Hard Way":

Open source software has had a carefree childhood, with two decades of freedom to grow and develop without much concern for security or accountability. However, around 2020, the landscape changed with major security breaches like SolarWinds and Log4Shell, which exposed the vulnerabilities of open source software. As a result, the open source community is being forced to grow up and take responsibility for its security and reliability.

The author predicts that the open source community will split into two groups: one that plays by the rules and meets the security and accountability standards required by enterprises, and another that continues to operate independently without these constraints. The first group, which the author calls the "subset," will need to demonstrate a "proof of life" and maintain a certain level of security and accountability to be considered trustworthy by enterprises. This will require a significant change in the way open source projects are maintained and supported.

The author argues that this change is not a threat to open source software, but rather a natural evolution of the community. While some projects may not meet the new standards, they will still be able to operate independently, and users will have the option to use them at their own risk. The author also notes that vendors will play a role in supporting and maintaining open source software, offering services like long-term support and security patches for a fee. This will provide users with a choice between using open source software for free, with the associated risks and responsibilities, or paying for supported and maintained versions.

Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

A leaked memo has tied a series of cyberattacks on Minnesota water utilities to Iran. The memo, circulated within the water industry, links the attacks to a hacking campaign first described by the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) in April as being carried out by "Iran-affiliated" hackers. The attacks targeted dozens of Minnesota water and wastewater utilities, resulting in disruptions and brief outages, but no evidence of water contamination or shortages has been reported.

The attacks are believed to have been carried out by Iranian state-sponsored hackers, with some cybersecurity firms pointing to CyberAv3ngers, a hacker group tied to the Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps, as the likely culprit. However, other firms suggest that another group, Handala, may be responsible. Despite the uncertainty, experts agree that the attacks bear the hallmarks of Iranian tradecraft and represent a concerning escalation of state-sponsored cyberattacks against civilian infrastructure.

The CISA has issued an advisory warning water utilities to take steps to protect themselves from similar attacks, including disconnecting programmable logic controllers (PLCs) from the internet and password-protecting access. Experts warn that the attacks could be just the beginning, with many other water utilities potentially vulnerable to similar attacks. The incident has raised concerns about the potential for state-sponsored hackers to disrupt critical infrastructure, and highlights the need for increased cybersecurity measures to protect against such threats.

Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

Weak Identity and Access Management (IAM) affects up to 98% of cloud environments, making it a significant threat to cloud security. Misconfiguration is a leading cause of cloud security risks, and a single error can result in public network access, unrotated keys, missing encryption, exposed services, and logging gaps. The US federal agencies now mandate baseline cloud configuration practices to mitigate these risks. According to a report by Intruder, more than two-thirds of midmarket organizations use multiple cloud providers, each with its own security model, terminology, and configuration settings.

The most common cloud security issues vary across Amazon Web Services (AWS), Azure, and Google Cloud, driven by each platform's architecture. AWS environments are most frequently affected by storage, network access, and identity management misconfigurations, while Azure's most common issues center on storage security and identity protection. Google Cloud is primarily affected by identity and access management (IAM) weaknesses. Weak identity controls, excessive permissions, and insecure or incomplete configurations remain the most common sources of cloud security risk across all three providers. Weak IAM controls and missing logging and alerting were the most widespread security issues, affecting 80% to 98% of accounts.

The prevalence of cloud security risks varies by organization size, with larger enterprises generally reporting fewer issues with permissive firewalls, exposed services, and weak encryption. However, IAM is the only category that becomes more challenging with scale, affecting 87% of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), 95% of midmarket organizations, and 98% of large enterprises. Remediation times also vary by organization size, with smaller organizations resolving cloud misconfigurations in an average of 8 to 16 days, while larger enterprises take around 10 days. Midmarket organizations often struggle with managing enterprise-scale cloud environments without dedicated security resources, making it essential to prioritize cloud security and implement robust IAM controls.

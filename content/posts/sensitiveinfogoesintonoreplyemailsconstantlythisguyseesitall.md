Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

Cory Solovewicz, a security researcher, has been receiving a massive amount of unwanted emails containing sensitive information, including injury reports, pizza orders, and company secrets. This is because he owns the domains noreply.us and noreply.net, which companies and organizations are inadvertently sending emails to, believing they are not being monitored. Solovewicz has received over 401,796 emails since December 2024, with an average of 699.99 emails per day, containing sensitive information that could be used maliciously.

The issue arises when companies send emails to placeholder addresses, such as [companyname]@noreply.net, thinking they are not going anywhere. However, Solovewicz's domains are catching these emails, revealing a large-scale problem of misconfigured internal systems. He has been warning businesses and organizations about the issue, encouraging them to fix the errors and misconfigurations. Solovewicz is not alone in this endeavor, as another researcher, Mike Sheward, has also been purchasing domains to limit the potential for malicious actors to exploit the issue.

Both researchers have been notifying companies about the problem, but the results have been mixed. While some organizations have fixed the issues, many others have not replied, and the sheer scale of the problem makes it a challenge to notify every impacted company. Solovewicz and Sheward are calling on companies to take responsibility for their systems and to use internal domains or the .invalid domain to avoid sending sensitive information to unmonitored email addresses. They are also warning that the problem may be larger than they have currently uncovered, with Solovewicz having scanned over 7,000 domains and finding 328 with catch-all inboxes configured.

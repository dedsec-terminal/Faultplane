A security researcher, Vangelis Stykas, has been tracking North Korean hackers for almost two years, gaining access to their systems and uncovering evidence of widespread breaches. Stykas found that 1,640 companies across 57 countries have been impacted by North Korean hacking operations, with around 700-800 of these organizations experiencing "really damaging" intrusions. The researcher accessed multiple command-and-control servers used by the hackers, giving him insight into their operations and allowing him to identify potential victims.

The North Korean hackers used a simple tactic of luring software developers with fake job offers, which would install malware on their machines. This technique, known as Contagious Interview, has been used since 2022 and has allowed the hackers to gain access to cryptocurrency wallets and other sensitive systems. Stykas has disclosed the incidents to the impacted companies, and some have responded by containing and remediating the breaches. However, many companies have not responded or have downplayed the incidents, and Stykas warns that the real concern is the companies that have not been named, which may still be vulnerable to ongoing hacking operations.

The scope of the North Korean hacking campaign is significant, with hundreds of skilled cyber operators and thousands of IT workers trained to support the country's priorities. The hackers have maintained a focus on gaining access to cryptocurrency wallets, but the risk of espionage and further breaches remains. Stykas' research highlights the importance of companies taking proactive steps to protect themselves against hacking operations and responding quickly and effectively in the event of a breach. The incident also underscores the need for increased awareness and cooperation to counter the threat posed by North Korean hackers and other malicious actors.

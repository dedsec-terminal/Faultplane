Here is a summary of the article in three concise paragraphs:

When under pressure, organizations often make critical mistakes during corporate investigations. According to Christine Gadsby, VP and Chief Security Advisor at BlackBerry, the first hours of an investigation are crucial, and decisions made during this time can impact the chain of custody, privilege, and regulatory judgment. Leaders often underestimate the importance of these initial hours, which can lead to errors that compromise the investigation.

Gadsby identifies four common mistakes that organizations make during corporate investigations. These include treating the investigation as a technical problem rather than a business event, losing track of sensitive conversations, assuming who has access to information, and limiting the chain of custody to devices and logs while ignoring other critical findings. These mistakes can have severe consequences, as evidenced by the over $2 billion in SEC penalties imposed on over 100 financial firms since 2021 due to missing records.

To avoid these mistakes, Gadsby recommends that organizations take a more structured approach to corporate investigations. This includes naming an incident commander, choosing communication channels with an audit trail, verifying participants, and documenting decisions as they are made. By following these guidelines, organizations can ensure that their investigations are thorough, transparent, and compliant with regulatory requirements.

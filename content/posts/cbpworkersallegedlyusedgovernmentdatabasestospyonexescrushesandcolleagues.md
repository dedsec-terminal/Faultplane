Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

Internal records obtained by WIRED reveal that US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) employees and contractors have been accused of misusing sensitive government databases for personal reasons, including spying on exes, crushes, and colleagues. The records, which span over a decade, contain hundreds of allegations of database abuse, including querying data to look up romantic interests, monitor family members, and expose personal information. In some cases, employees allegedly provided intelligence to suspected smugglers or drug-trafficking organizations.

The allegations include cases of CBP officers using government databases to contact flight attendants, ask people out, or track coworkers' cell phones. One employee allegedly used ad-tech-derived location data to track several coworkers' phones, which appears to be the first known internal abuse case involving DHS use of such data. The records also show that employees were accused of providing law-enforcement information to people suspected of criminal activity, including a case where an officer allegedly advised smugglers which lane to use at the border.

The allegations highlight concerns about the abuse of power and the lack of accountability within CBP. The agency has a history of impunity and abuse of people's civil and human rights, according to Laura Rivera, an attorney with Just Futures Law. The use of digital surveillance tools is supposed to help officers screen travelers and investigate crimes more efficiently, but the records reveal that sensitive data collected for law-enforcement purposes was often weaponized against private individuals. The CBP has stated that it takes allegations of misconduct seriously and works to uphold the rule of law, but the lack of transparency and accountability in the cases raises concerns about the agency's ability to prevent such abuses in the future.

Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

A 26-year-old Canadian man, Connor Riley Moucka, has pleaded guilty to computer fraud and conspiracy to hack and extort over 165 organizations that used the cloud provider Snowflake. Between February and October 2024, Moucka and his co-conspirators used stolen login credentials to steal cloud-hosted data, targeting companies that did not enforce multi-factor authentication. The hackers extorted or attempted to extort well-known companies, including TicketMaster, Lending Tree, and Neiman Marcus.

Moucka, who used the monikers "Judische" and "Waifu," admitted to stealing billions of sensitive customer records, including call and text history records, banking information, and personally identifiable information. He and his co-conspirators made over $2.5 million in ransom payments and threatened to publish stolen data online. Moucka also harassed government officials and security researchers who were tracking him down. One of his co-conspirators, Cameron "Kiberphant0m" Wagenius, a US Army soldier, pleaded guilty to extorting AT&T and Verizon and is set to be sentenced in September 2026.

Moucka faces a mandatory minimum penalty of two years in prison and a maximum penalty of 30 years in prison. His case highlights the importance of using unique passwords and enabling multi-factor authentication to prevent cyber attacks. The investigation also shed light on the activities of another alleged co-conspirator, John Erin Binns, who fled the US after being indicted for his role in a 2021 breach at T-Mobile. Binns, who obtained Turkish citizenship, may be able to avoid extradition due to Turkish law. The case demonstrates the need for individuals and organizations to prioritize cybersecurity and take proactive measures to protect themselves from cyber threats.

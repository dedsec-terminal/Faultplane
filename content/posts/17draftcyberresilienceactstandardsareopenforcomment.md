The European Union's Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) requires companies selling connected products, such as smart home devices and wearables, to demonstrate compliance by the end of 2027. However, the law does not provide technical details on how to achieve compliance, leaving manufacturers to figure it out on their own. To address this, 17 draft standards have been released for public comment, which will provide the necessary technical details for manufacturers to follow.

These draft standards, developed by Standards Developing Organisations, cover higher-risk products with digital elements and will provide a presumption of conformity with the CRA if followed. This means that regulators will treat products as compliant with the law unless proven otherwise. The drafts are open for comment from 41 member organisations, including national standardisation bodies and societal partners, until mid-September to mid-November 2026. This comment period allows for feedback from various stakeholders, including consumer, environmental, labor, and small-business groups.

The CRA's compliance requirements extend beyond manufacturers to importers, distributors, service providers, and developers of commercially available hardware and software. Small and medium-sized firms may face challenges in navigating the compliance process, including determining which standard applies to their product and accessing funding programs. By commenting on the draft standards, stakeholders can help shape the final version and ensure that the CRA is effective in promoting cybersecurity resilience. Manufacturers who wait until the deadline to review the standards may find that the final version has already been settled by others, highlighting the importance of participating in the comment period.

The Swiss government's Federal Office of Information Technology, Systems and Telecommunication (BIT) recently suffered a cybersecurity breach, compromising around 200 accounts on its Microsoft SharePoint servers. The incident occurred when hackers exploited vulnerabilities in the SharePoint software, which were disclosed by Microsoft in mid-July. BIT's security specialists detected unusual activity on the servers on July 28 and promptly blocked internet access to the platform.

An investigation revealed that the login credentials for several accounts, including both user and technical accounts, had been compromised. BIT immediately reset the passwords for all affected accounts to prevent further unauthorized access. The agency believes that the attackers exploited vulnerabilities in the SharePoint software, possibly using either a privilege escalation vulnerability (CVE-2026-56164) or a remote code execution flaw (CVE-2026-50522). The attackers are described as "previously unknown actors," and no group has claimed responsibility for the attack.

BIT is working with the Federal Office for Cybersecurity (BACS) and Microsoft to analyze the incident. Fortunately, no confidential information or sensitive personal data is stored on the affected SharePoint platform, and there is currently no evidence of data leakage beyond the compromised login credentials. The agency has reported the incident to the relevant authorities and shared technical indicators with operators of essential infrastructure. Alternative methods have been established for employees to access and share documents, minimizing the impact of the breach.

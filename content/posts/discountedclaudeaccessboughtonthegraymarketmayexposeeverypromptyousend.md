Researchers at Okta have discovered a gray market for discounted access to AI models, including Claude, where users can purchase "unlimited" tokens at a lower cost. However, this discounted access comes with significant risks, including the potential for service providers to have full visibility into user prompts, which could lead to data leaks or sales. The demand for these services is driven by users seeking access to AI models that are restricted due to regulatory or provider limitations, particularly in China.

Okta identified several services, including Poison Claude and Ecomagent, that offer discounted access to AI models. These services often use fraudulent account registrations, taking advantage of free trials or credits, to provide access to users. However, the services can be unstable, and access can disappear without notice if the underlying accounts are shut down. Additionally, the services may leave users' prompts and data exposed, as they are forwarded to the AI models through the service providers' servers.

The use of these gray market services appears to be driven by Chinese users seeking access to restricted AI models. Okta's research found that over 105,000 brute-force attempts were made to sign up for a free trial of an AI video company's service, with many of the attempts coming from IP addresses tied to Chinese VPN providers. The company also found that scammers are cashing in on the demand, selling access to AI services on cybercriminal forums. To counter this, Okta recommends that AI companies implement robust fraud controls and security measures to protect user data and prevent unauthorized access to their services.

Satellite images have revealed the rapid expansion of suspected scam compounds in Myanmar, with over two dozen new sites emerging in the past six months. The compounds, often operated by Chinese organized crime groups, are responsible for vast portions of global online scamming, including cryptocurrency and romance scams targeting victims worldwide. The images show land being flattened, forests razed, and dozens of buildings and infrastructure being constructed to support large-scale scam operations.

The growth of these compounds suggests that a purported crackdown on the sites last year had little impact on the lucrative scamming industry. Despite raids by Myanmar's military junta, which destroyed some compounds and seized equipment, the scammers have simply relocated and expanded their operations. Many of the new sites are in remote locations, and their layout and infrastructure are consistent with known scam compounds. The use of foreign internet service providers, including Elon Musk's Starlink, has also been detected at these sites.

The scamming industry has become a global crisis, with hundreds of thousands of trafficking victims being forced to work in these compounds. The victims, lured by false promises of work, are subjected to beatings and have their passports taken, making it difficult for them to escape. Despite international sanctions and crackdowns, the scammers continue to operate with impunity, with little being done to target the criminal entities behind the operations. Experts warn that the problem is growing, with new victims being brought into the compounds and the scammers adapting to evade detection.

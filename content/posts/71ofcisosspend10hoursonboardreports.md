A recent report by Pulse Security AI, "The CISO-Board Communication Gap," highlights the challenges Chief Information Security Officers (CISOs) face in communicating with their boards. The report found that 71% of CISOs spend over 10 hours preparing for board or audit committee presentations, which are typically held quarterly. This significant time burden is due to the difficulty of translating technical findings into business language, making it hard to provide evidence that security controls and architecture reduce business risk.

The report also reveals that confidence in board reporting remains low, with only 12.5% of CISOs being very confident that their board accurately understands the true state of the security program after a presentation. This lack of confidence stems from the fact that many organizations lack a formally defined cyber risk appetite, and board members often bring external information into discussions without a clear framework for understanding cyber risk. As a result, CISOs are calling for simpler data delivery, better frameworks, and better context to support their reporting.

The report identifies several governance gaps that limit board oversight, including the reliance on instinct rather than measurement, limited access to private sessions with the board or audit committee, and the lack of predefined board-level cyber incident escalation thresholds. To address these gaps, CISOs need better tools and operating layers to support their reporting, including automated threat and vulnerability analysis, automated data aggregation, and tools for translating findings into business impact. By addressing these challenges, CISOs can provide more effective reporting and support more informed decision-making by their boards.

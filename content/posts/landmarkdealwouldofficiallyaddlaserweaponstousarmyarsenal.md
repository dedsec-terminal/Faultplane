The EHEL is designed to be compact, powerful, and able to knock out one-way attack drones, making it a valuable asset in the face of increasing drone threats.

The development of laser weapons has been a long and challenging process, with numerous attempts over the past 50 years yielding limited success. However, recent advancements in technology have made lasers more viable, with improvements in fiber lasers, semiconductor diodes, and machine vision. The EHEL is expected to be an operational "program of record," indicating a wider cultural acceptance of the technology as a military capability. This designation will signal to contractors to ramp up production, paving the way for the widespread adoption of laser weapons.

Despite the promise of laser weapons, there are still limitations and challenges to be addressed. Lasers can be affected by weather conditions like rain and fog, and they require a line of sight to the target. Additionally, lasers can pose a danger to aircraft and satellites if not properly controlled. Experts caution that lasers should be used in ways that complement traditional munitions, rather than replacing them entirely. The EHEL is expected to be used to counter low-cost, high-volume drone threats, where traditional interceptors may be too expensive or impractical. With the contract signing imminent, the US Army is poised to take a significant step forward in its adoption of laser technology.

Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

Microsoft has released updates to fix at least 398 security vulnerabilities in its Windows operating systems and supported software. This includes one weakness that is already being actively exploited and two others that were publicly detailed prior to the update. The majority of the flaws are not critical, but 42 of them have been given a "critical" rating, meaning they could allow malware or attackers to gain remote control over a Windows computer with little to no help from the user.

The use of artificial intelligence (AI) has been attributed to the recent surge in vulnerability discoveries, with Microsoft and other software makers increasing their patch volumes and cadence. However, while AI is good at finding security holes, it is not yet reliable at fixing them. Researchers have found that AI-generated patches often fail to fix the flaw or introduce new weaknesses. As a result, human involvement is still necessary to test and improve the suggested fixes.

The increasing number of patches has prompted some organizations to try to patch faster, but security experts warn against rushing to apply updates. They recommend testing fixes before deploying them in production environments and ensuring that the updates will not negatively impact systems. With the rise of AI-powered vulnerability discovery, the landscape of cybersecurity is changing, and it remains to be seen whether AI will ultimately be a net positive or negative force in the field. For now, users are advised to exercise caution and carefully consider their patching strategies to stay safe.

Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

A new free service called DecryptAds has been launched to help individuals understand who is tracking them online. The service scrapes and correlates adtech data from publicly available files, including ads.txt, app-ads.txt, and buyers.json/sellers.json, to provide a comprehensive picture of the entities that are tracking users. This information can be used to identify malicious ads, detect data brokers, and uncover potential security and privacy threats.

DecryptAds allows users to search for specific websites or apps and view the list of ad partners and data brokers associated with them. For example, a search for espn.com reveals 143 ad partners and 19 registered data broker domains, with almost half of the data brokers collecting geolocation data from visitors. The service also flags adtech partners based in "geo-risk" areas, such as China and Russia, and provides a dossier on each company, including their ownership structure and relationships with other websites and apps.

The service has already uncovered some disturbing trends, including the presence of adtech firms based in Russia and China on popular websites, such as espn.com and opera.com. DecryptAds also provides a "quiet removals" feed, which tracks the removal of suspicious advertisers from ad exchanges, and a "legal dossier" lookup, which provides detailed information on the ownership and relationships of specific domains and apps. The service aims to provide transparency and accountability in the adtech industry, which has been criticized for its lack of oversight and vulnerability to malicious activity.

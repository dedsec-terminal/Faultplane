Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

As AI agents become increasingly prevalent in organizations, security teams are realizing that simply having visibility into these agents is not enough. AI agent security is evolving through a maturity curve, from adoption to visibility to control. However, enforcing least privilege for AI agents is proving to be more challenging than expected, with various approaches being explored, such as prompt filtering and identity-layer access controls. Understanding the intent of AI agents is crucial to securing them, and this requires a more nuanced approach than traditional security measures.

The risk associated with AI agents lies not in their mere presence, but in their ability to operate across systems without consistent identity, intent, ownership, and enforcement. AI agents are not passive entities; they can reason, plan, and take action without human intervention, accessing data and invoking APIs. To mitigate these risks, security teams need to move beyond mere visibility and focus on enforcement, which involves defining rules that govern what agents can do, when, and under what conditions. This requires a deep understanding of the agent's intent, ownership, consumers, identities, permissions, and usage patterns.

To achieve effective AI agent enforcement, security teams need to adopt a more contextual and identity-centric approach. This involves correlating information across various dimensions, such as ownership, intent, access, and usage, to define meaningful controls. A unified control plane is necessary to govern AI agents across the enterprise, enabling discovery, understanding, and enforcement of consistent rules. Security leaders can start building this operating model now by treating AI agent visibility as a starting point, rather than the finish line, and focusing on enforcement and governance to ensure that AI agents are used securely and responsibly.

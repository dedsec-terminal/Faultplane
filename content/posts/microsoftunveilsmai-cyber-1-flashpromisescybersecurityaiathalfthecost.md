Microsoft has introduced MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, a security-focused AI model designed to identify vulnerabilities in complex codebases. This model is built into MDASH, the company's multi-agent vulnerability identification and remediation system, and has undergone rigorous testing, including review by Microsoft's AI Red Team and assessment by an outside party. MAI-Cyber-1-Flash is Microsoft's first AI model specifically built for cybersecurity work, and it aims to help organizations harden their software against increasingly powerful AI-powered attacks.

According to Microsoft, MAI-Cyber-1-Flash outperformed other leading models, including Mythos, Gemini, and GPT, on the CyberGym benchmark, scoring 12 points higher than Mythos. When combined with MDASH, MAI-Cyber-1-Flash delivers world-class performance at 50% of the cost of leading models, making it a cost-effective solution for organizations. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella highlighted the model's potential to revolutionize cybersecurity, stating that it can help unlock the benefits of AI while ensuring the security of software.

In addition to MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, Microsoft also introduced Project Perception, an agentic security system built on MDASH. Project Perception is a continuously learning system that brings together signals, context, models, and specialized agents to reason, prioritize, and act at machine speed while keeping humans in control. The project will enter public preview on August 3, 2026, and Microsoft plans to expand it with additional specialized security agents over time. With these new developments, Microsoft aims to provide organizations with powerful tools to enhance their cybersecurity capabilities and stay ahead of emerging threats.

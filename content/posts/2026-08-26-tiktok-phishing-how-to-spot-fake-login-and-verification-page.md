### Executive Summary
Malwarebytes Labs reports that scammers create counterfeit TikTok login pages, pop-up warnings, and fake verification offers to lure users into entering credentials. The sites mimic the official interface, prompting users to confirm their account or pay for a "verification" badge. Once credentials are entered, attackers can hijack the account, steal personal data, or use it for further phishing. The article outlines visual cues to identify legitimate TikTok pages and advises users to verify URLs, look for HTTPS and the official TikTok logo, and avoid unsolicited verification prompts.

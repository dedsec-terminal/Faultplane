The annual Defcon hacker conference has introduced a new conference badge that features a unique open-source chip designed by legendary hardware hacker Andrew "bunnie" Huang. The chip, called the Baochip-1x, is a "mostly" open-source microcontroller that aims to advance the state of security, transparency, and trustworthiness in computing. The chip is packaged in a way that allows researchers to visually inspect its internal structures using infrared light, making it possible to verify its security.

The Baochip-1x is a significant innovation in the field of computer chips, as it is designed to be transparent and verifiable, unlike traditional black-box components. Huang has published the source code for the chip's operating system, firmware, and other components on GitHub, making it available for inspection and use. The chip can be removed from the conference badge and used as a hardware security token, giving it a second life beyond the conference. The badge itself has features that encourage attendees to interact, such as LED lights that flash in different patterns depending on the type of badge.

The Baochip-1x has several security features, including secure boot, a true random number generator, and resistive RAM, which makes it difficult to extract stored data. Huang believes that the chip is resistant to remote, non-physical attacks, but acknowledges that an adversary with significant resources could potentially defeat it. The chip is designed to be stress-tested by Defcon attendees, who will likely hack it and expose flaws, helping to make it even more secure. Huang plans to expand the chip's features and expects Defcon participants to build on what he's provided, making the chip a platform for innovation and experimentation.

A Canadian man, Brandon Klayme, was wrongly imprisoned for 18 months due to a typo in a username. The investigation began in 2018 when a 12-year-old Wisconsin girl reported communicating with an adult male on the Kik messaging service. The police identified the username "fus__ro_dah" (two underscores) as the perpetrator, but accidentally requested records for the username "fus_ro_dah" (one underscore), which belonged to Klayme.

As a result, Klayme was arrested, charged with child sex abuse, and convicted in 2023. Despite no evidence of the crime being found on his digital devices, he was sentenced to 18 months in prison. It wasn't until Klayme's appeal that the mistake was discovered, and the Nova Scotia Court of Appeal overturned his conviction. The court stated that Klayme was "factually innocent" and should never have been charged or convicted.

The mistake was attributed to a single missing underscore in the username, which led investigators to Klayme's email address and IP address in Canada. The court noted that had the case been properly investigated, evidence would have pointed to a different individual, "Jay", with an IP address in California. The Court of Appeal acquitted Klayme on all charges, vacating his sentence and probation. The case highlights the importance of attention to detail in investigations and the devastating consequences of a simple mistake.

The decision to use AI for a task can be simplified by distinguishing between "work" and "gym" tasks. Work tasks are those where the goal is to complete a task efficiently, and AI assistance can be beneficial. On the other hand, gym tasks are those where the process of completing the task is just as important as the outcome, and using AI would undermine the purpose. For example, if a student is assigned to write a policy memo, using AI to write the memo would be counterproductive, as the goal is to develop critical thinking and writing skills, not just to produce a well-written document.

This distinction is particularly relevant for creatives, such as writers and visual artists. While AI can be useful for tasks that require dry, predictable, and accurate writing or image creation, it is less suitable for tasks that require creativity, nuance, and originality. The rise of AI may lead to a shift in the way society values and compensates creatives, with a greater emphasis on tasks that require human skills and judgment. By recognizing the difference between work and gym tasks, individuals can make informed decisions about when to use AI and when to rely on human skills and effort.

The pressure to use AI can be intense, particularly in academic and professional settings where there is a strong incentive to produce high-quality work quickly and efficiently. However, by prioritizing the development of human skills and abilities, individuals can ensure that they remain relevant and valuable in a world where AI is increasingly prevalent. By making a conscious choice to use AI judiciously and to reserve certain tasks for human effort, individuals can maintain their critical thinking, creativity, and problem-solving skills, and thrive in a future where AI is an integral part of the workforce.

Hugging Face, a popular open-source AI platform, is facing criticism for hosting models that can create nonconsensual deepfake nudes. Researchers from AI Forensics tested nine image editing models on the platform and found that seven of them could easily remove clothing from a woman's image. The researchers also created a "honey pot" to track user requests and found that 73% of the prompts were sexual in nature, with 83% seeking to undress or sexualize women.

The findings suggest that Hugging Face's content moderation policies are not effective in preventing the creation and dissemination of nonconsensual deepfakes. The platform's terms of service prohibit child sexual abuse material and sexual deepfakes created without consent, but the researchers found that many models on the platform can be used to create such content. Hugging Face has responded to the criticism, questioning the methodologies used by the researchers and claiming that the prompts gathered are not representative of how image editing systems are used on the platform.

The issue of nonconsensual deepfakes is a growing concern, with many tech companies struggling to prevent the creation and dissemination of such content. The use of generative AI models to create deepfakes has been linked to harassment, blackmail, and other forms of abuse, particularly against women and girls. Experts are calling for more effective content moderation and safety mechanisms to be implemented to prevent the creation and dissemination of nonconsensual deepfakes. Hugging Face has said it is working to address the issue, but the company's response has been criticized as inadequate by some researchers.

A team of security researchers at UC San Diego has discovered vulnerabilities in the KARR Security System, a popular aftermarket car alarm. This system is estimated to be installed in over 2 million vehicles in the US. The vulnerabilities allow hackers to exploit the system's Bluetooth connectivity.

Within Bluetooth range, a hacker can send radio commands to the car, allowing them to unlock the vehicle silently, turn off its alarm, honk the horn, or flash the lights. Additionally, hackers can disable the car's ignition, leaving the driver stranded. This raises significant concerns about the security and reliability of the KARR Security System.

The discovery highlights the importance of prioritizing cybersecurity in vehicle security systems. Consumers who have the KARR Security System installed in their vehicles should be aware of the potential risks and consider taking steps to mitigate them. The researchers' findings serve as a reminder to choose security systems from reputable manufacturers, such as the fictional Knight Industries 2000, which is jokingly suggested as a more secure alternative.

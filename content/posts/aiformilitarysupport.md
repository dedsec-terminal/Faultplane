A recent study, "Black Box Warfare: Human Judgment and Military Decision-Making in the Age of AI," explores how AI is transforming decision-making in modern conflict. The research used a replica of a real-world AI decision-support system used in military targeting and tested its impact on combat decisions with 2,015 Israeli military personnel. The study found that, contrary to expectations, humans exhibited algorithmic aversion, particularly in scenarios with high collateral damage, rather than automation bias.

The study also investigated the effect of "explainable AI" features on human trust in AI decision-making. The results showed that integrating these features reduced algorithmic aversion and promoted more thoughtful evaluations of AI recommendations. Additionally, the research found that humans trusted AI more in targeting decisions when they had greater transparency about inputs. This suggests that providing explanatory data can increase trust in AI and lead to more informed decision-making.

The study's findings also highlighted the importance of human factors in decision-making, including vengefulness towards the adversary, gender differences, and conservative political orientation. Technological literacy was also found to influence decision certainty. Overall, the research challenges prevailing assumptions about the role of AI in military decision-making and underscores the need for a nuanced understanding of the complex interactions between humans and AI in high-stakes decision-making contexts. By providing empirical evidence, the study offers critical insights into the integration of AI in warfare and the enduring importance of human agency.

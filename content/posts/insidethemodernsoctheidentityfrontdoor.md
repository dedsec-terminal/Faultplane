Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

The modern Security Operations Center (SOC) faces a significant challenge in the form of identity-based attacks. According to the 2026 Unit 42 Global Incident Response Report, nearly 90% of incidents investigated involved identity weaknesses, with 65% of initial access activity involving identity-based techniques such as credential theft, multifactor authentication (MFA) manipulation, and social engineering. Attackers are increasingly using compromised identities to gain access to enterprise environments, making it essential for SOCs to prioritize identity context and behavioral analysis.

Once inside, attackers establish persistence, elevate privileges, and move laterally across environments, often disguising their activities as legitimate administrative behavior. The attacker's playbook typically involves social engineering, phishing, and MFA fatigue attacks to gain initial access, followed by exploitation of identity weaknesses to expand their foothold. The objective of these attacks can range from ransomware deployment to data theft, financial fraud, or long-term persistence. To detect and respond to these attacks, SOCs need to unify security telemetry, automate correlation, and provide behavioral context to distinguish legitimate users from compromised accounts.

To effectively respond to identity-driven attacks, SOC leaders should prioritize identity context, reduce manual investigation, and continuously improve detection capabilities. This can be achieved by consolidating telemetry and investigations into a unified view, refining detections and correlation rules, and dedicating time for threat hunting. Unit 42's Managed Services team uses the Cortex SecOps platform to unify security telemetry and provide expert-led Managed Detection and Response (MDR), proactive threat hunting, and continuous SOC engineering to help organizations identify identity-driven attacks earlier and respond with greater confidence. By adopting a unified and automated approach, SOCs can stay ahead of emerging identity-based threats and protect their organizations from business impact.

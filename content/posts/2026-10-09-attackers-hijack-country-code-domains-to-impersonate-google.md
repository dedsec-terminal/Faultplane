### Executive Summary
Cybercriminals compromised three country-code top-level domains (ccTLDs) to obtain TLS certificates, enabling them to impersonate trusted sites such as Google. By hijacking the DNS infrastructure of these ccTLDs, attackers can issue certificates that appear legitimate, facilitating phishing and man‑in‑the‑middle attacks. The incident highlights the vulnerability of ccTLD registries to certificate abuse and the need for stricter domain and certificate oversight.

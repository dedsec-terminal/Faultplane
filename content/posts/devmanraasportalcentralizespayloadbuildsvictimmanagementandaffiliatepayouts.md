Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

The DevMan ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) operation has been found to have a dedicated web platform that allows affiliates to build payloads, manage earnings, and oversee various aspects related to victims. The platform, tracked by Swiss cybersecurity company PRODAFT under the name Funky Mantis, offers a range of features including build generation, finance, victim chat, support, and payout functions. The service also integrates access brokerage with ransomware deployment, allowing administrators to offer country-specific networks and impose completion windows.

DevMan first emerged in April 2025 as an affiliate for other RaaS operations, but later shifted to its own operation. The group's ransomware has been linked to DragonForce, and its operators have claimed to have developed a specialized SCADA locker to target industrial control systems. The group's operations have been tracked by various security researchers, who have identified 184 victims to date, with nearly 50 located in the US. The group's affiliate portal has undergone several updates, with the latest version featuring support for structured victim records, team creation, and deadline tracking.

The DevMan operation has been found to have a governance model that reduces affiliate autonomy, while giving administrators the power to enforce operational tempo and protect revenue. The group's stated targeting policy allows affiliates to strike entities outside of certain countries, while excluding others, such as child-related healthcare businesses. The group's locker has been analyzed, and its functions include privilege checking, security-control impairment, and multi-threaded encryption. Security researchers have warned organizations to take precautions, such as prohibiting service and backup accounts from interactive VPN login, to prevent DevMan attacks.

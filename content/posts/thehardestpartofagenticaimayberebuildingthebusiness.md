Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

Organizations are expecting AI agents to drive productivity and growth, but few have the necessary processes and workflows to realize these benefits. According to Deloitte's research, about half of surveyed leaders understand how AI agents will affect their future operating model, but three main challenges limit wider adoption: lack of unified data, limited trust and governance, and high integration costs. Additionally, issues related to data, decision-making, governance, and workforce readiness are constraining AI adoption, with few organizations prepared across most areas of the business.

Leaders expect AI agents to change business processes and the workforce over the next four years, with processes being redesigned to support real-time decision-making and greater autonomy. However, only 16% of leaders say their organizations' processes are ready for agentic AI, and even fewer (5%) describe them as highly prepared. Executives attribute the lack of readiness to poorly documented processes, fragmented data and systems, and limited AI expertise. Many organizations are introducing AI agents into existing workflows instead of redesigning processes from the ground up, which may offer short-term payback but may not lead to long-term value.

The adoption of agentic AI is expected to lead to significant job disruption, with 43% of executives expecting a lot to extreme job disruption within the next 12 to 18 months. Routine and structured tasks are likely to be automated, while creative and strategic work will continue to require human involvement. Organizations are preparing workers for this shift through AI literacy programs and targeted training, but half of surveyed leaders say their organizations are not investing enough in workforce changes. Governance will play a key role in defining working arrangements between employees and AI agents, including decision-making authority and task allocation.

Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article about UNC6671 vishing attacks:

A cyber attack group known as UNC6671 has been targeting financial services, private equity, and professional services using voice phishing (vishing) attacks. The group poses as IT help desk staff and contacts employees on their personal mobile devices, tricking them into revealing their login credentials and multi-factor authentication (MFA) tokens. The attackers then use this information to gain access to enterprise cloud environments and SaaS applications, including Microsoft 365 and Okta.

UNC6671 has been operating under multiple extortion brands, including Redact, Pink, Helix, and Falcon, and has been tracked by Google and Mandiant. The group's tactics include using credential harvesting panels, compromised email accounts, and spoofed phone numbers to initiate password resets and gain access to sensitive information. The attackers have been able to extort millions of dollars from their victims, with initial ransom demands reaching up to $3 million. To counter the threat, organizations are recommended to enforce phishing-resistant MFA, integrate SaaS applications with SSO, and monitor IdP logs for suspicious activity.

The UNC6671 group's operations have been characterized as a decentralized corporate network, using shared infrastructure across multiple public-facing brands to manage negotiations and insulate their operations. The group's use of multiple brands and tactics has made it difficult to track and attribute the attacks. However, researchers believe that the group may be affiliated with other threat actors, such as ShinyHunters or Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters (SLH). To protect against these types of attacks, organizations are advised to implement robust security measures, including phishing-resistant MFA, session controls, and security tooling to alert on suspicious activity.

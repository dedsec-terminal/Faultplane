### Executive Summary

Researchers from The DFIR Report, in partnership with Proofpoint, identified a new, resilient variant of the Interlock ransomware group’s remote access trojan (RAT). Unlike the earlier JavaScript‑based Interlock RAT (NodeSnake), the new malware is written in PHP and has been deployed in a widespread campaign since May 2025. The discovery highlights the group’s evolving tactics and the need for organizations to monitor for PHP‑based RAT activity. No specific defensive guidance was detailed in the report.

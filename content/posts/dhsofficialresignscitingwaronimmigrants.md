Marc Rosenblum, the Department of Homeland Security's (DHS) top statistician, has resigned, citing the Trump administration's "war on immigrants". As the executive director of the Office of Homeland Security Statistics and a deputy assistant secretary, Rosenblum was responsible for collating data on various aspects of DHS activities, including immigration, cybersecurity, and trafficking investigations. His resignation is notable, as few officials have left the department and spoken out against its direction under the Trump administration.

Rosenblum's office was tasked with consolidating data from 22 different component agencies within DHS, providing a clearer picture of the department's activities. However, under the second Trump term, the office's website has not been updated since February 2025, and data released by the department has been sparse and unreliable. Rosenblum had promised to release data more quickly and with greater granularity, but his efforts were hindered by the administration's lack of transparency. His resignation highlights the challenges faced by career officials in providing accurate and unbiased information under the current administration.

Rosenblum's criticism of the Trump administration's immigration policies is not new. In 2015, he warned that mass deportation efforts could be risky, expensive, and cruel, and would likely have negative consequences for the US economy. His warnings have been borne out by the administration's actions, which have been marked by controversy and a lack of transparency. Rosenblum's resignation is a significant blow to the department, and his criticism of the administration's policies is a rare example of a high-ranking official speaking out against the direction of the department.

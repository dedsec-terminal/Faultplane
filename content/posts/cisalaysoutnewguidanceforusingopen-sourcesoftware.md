The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has released a guide titled "Open Source Software: Security Principles and Practices" to help federal agencies manage the security of open-source software. The guide recommends that agencies treat open-source software like any other software asset, assessing its security before adoption and monitoring it throughout its lifecycle. This includes choosing actively maintained projects, understanding licenses, and maintaining an inventory of open-source components.

The guide also provides recommendations for contributing to open-source projects, including sharing improvements and security fixes with the wider community. Agencies are encouraged to consider open-source development and publication from the start, unless there are legal, security, or operational reasons to prevent release. Before publishing a project, agencies should review it for sensitive information and follow secure software development practices. Additionally, the guide advises agencies to maintain projects by publishing updates, addressing security issues, and clearly communicating when software is no longer supported.

The guide also addresses the evaluation of open-source AI systems, noting that they should be evaluated differently from open-source software. This is because AI models can be released under an open-source license without making their training data publicly available, making it difficult to establish a model's provenance or assess its development process. The guide recommends that organizations determine whether they have sufficient visibility into how an open-source AI system was developed before deploying it, and if not, to treat it as proprietary software with incomplete provenance and apply stricter risk management practices.

Some municipalities, such as Denver, Colorado, are replacing their Flock license plate surveillance systems with Axon cameras. However, this switch may not necessarily reduce the amount of personal data being collected, as Axon cameras are also effective at gathering detailed information beyond just license plate numbers.

The author argues that switching from Flock to Axon is not a significant improvement in terms of privacy, as both systems are designed to collect and store personal data. This change is likened to a gambler switching from one platform to another, with the underlying issue of surveillance remaining unchanged.

The use of Axon cameras may even introduce new concerns, such as the potential for increased surveillance capabilities, including drones and other technologies. The author suggests that the data collected by these systems will continue to exist, regardless of the provider, and that the switch from Flock to Axon is merely a cosmetic change, with the underlying issue of surveillance and data collection remaining unaddressed.

Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the article:

Meta, the company owned by Mark Zuckerberg, has been running dozens of paid ads containing AI-generated child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and images of minors alongside sexually suggestive statements. These ads, which were targeted at people in the US, UK, and several European countries, were discovered by researchers at the Tech Transparency Project (TTP) in Meta's ad library. The ads, which reached thousands of accounts, linked to so-called nudify or undressing apps and were approved by Meta's review process, allowing the company to collect ad revenue.

The TTP researchers found over 50 image and video ads containing abusive content, which were published between November last year and August. The ads often ran for several days and targeted men, with some ads using thumbnails of children with text overlay suggesting explicit content. When clicked, the ads played video clips of adults involved in sexual acts, with some ads morphing into child sexual abuse material. Meta removed the ads after being contacted by WIRED, citing a violation of its policies on child sexual exploitation and adult nudity.

The discovery of these ads raises significant concerns about Meta's ability to detect and prevent child exploitation on its platforms. The TTP's director, Katie Paul, questioned how seriously Meta is taking child exploitation in paid advertisements, given that some of the ads had been previously removed but reappeared with identical content. Meta has stated that it takes a "zero tolerance" approach to child sexual exploitation, but the company's systems appear to have failed to detect and prevent these ads from running. The incident highlights the need for improved detection and prevention measures to combat the growing problem of AI-generated child sexual abuse material online.

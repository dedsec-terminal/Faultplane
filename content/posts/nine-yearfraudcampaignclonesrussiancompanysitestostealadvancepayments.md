Here is a summary of the article in three concise paragraphs:

A large-scale fraud campaign has been uncovered, involving the creation of clone websites of major Russian companies to steal advance payments from international firms. The campaign, which has been ongoing since 2017, targets organizations in the business-to-business (B2B) sector and international trade, with a focus on the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS) countries. The threat actors set up fake websites, often with lookalike domain names, to deceive potential clients into making payments for goods that do not exist.

The scheme involves creating fake websites that mimic the legitimate company websites, with altered contact details leading to the attackers. The threat actors also use cold calls, phishing email campaigns, and fraudulent corporate websites to initiate contact with potential customers. Once negotiations reach the final stage, the customer's communications are taken over by the fraudsters, who send commercial offers, contracts, and invoices with bogus bank details. One victim, an Azerbaijani company, lost $150,000 in a fraudulent transaction. The campaign has been linked to nearly 100 counterfeit domains, with common DNS records, IP addresses, and registration data indicating a single coordinated effort.

To mitigate against the threat, organizations are advised to exercise due diligence on business partners using trusted sources and government business registries. This includes verifying contact information and payment details before transferring funds, checking the supplier's website domain and registration date, and confirming the legitimacy of subsidiaries. The campaign's level of replication is a concern, with the threat actors copying fraud warnings from legitimate company websites and replacing them with fake ones. By taking these precautions, businesses can protect themselves from falling victim to this sophisticated and long-running fraud campaign.

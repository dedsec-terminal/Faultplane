Here are three concise paragraphs summarizing the IT threat evolution in Q2 2026:

Kaspersky products blocked nearly 400 million attacks from online resources in Q2 2026, with Web Anti-Virus responding to 52 million unique links and File Anti-Virus blocking over 16 million malicious objects. Ransomware attacks continued to be a significant threat, with 2538 new variants discovered and over 71,000 users experiencing attacks. The Qilin ransomware group was particularly active, accounting for 14.57% of all ransomware victims whose data was published on threat actors' data leak sites.

The quarter also saw notable developments in the ransomware landscape, including Microsoft's dismantling of an illicit malware-signing service used by ransomware operators. Additionally, CISA confirmed that a Windows vulnerability known as BlueHammer is being actively exploited in ransomware attacks. Check Point linked the zero-day exploitation of a critical vulnerability to the Qilin ransomware group, and researchers discovered that the PayoutsKing group is using the legitimate QEMU emulator to deploy hidden virtual machines on compromised hosts. The most prolific ransomware gangs included Qilin, Akira, and DragonForce RaaS.

In other threat areas, Kaspersky solutions detected 6067 new miner variants, almost twice the number from the previous quarter, with over 213,000 unique users targeted by miners. Attacks on macOS devices included a new attack by the GlassWorm stealer, a supply chain compromise involving the npm package art-template, and the discovery of a new backdoor family called FlutterShell. The top 10 countries and territories attacked by ransomware and miners were also identified, with South Korea and Mali being the most targeted by ransomware and miners, respectively. IoT threat statistics showed a breakdown of attacked services by protocol, with the majority of attacks targeting Telnet and HTTP services.

The development of Artificial Intelligence (AI) represents a significant technological revolution, comparable to the industrial revolution, with the potential to transform society, businesses, and governments. However, despite its potential, many Americans are concerned that AI is moving too fast and will have a negative impact on society. This confluence of technological revolution and public distrust requires urgent discussion and a proper framing of the issues.

The key issue is not whether AI can be developed in a non-exploitative way, but rather whether our existing social and economic systems are failing to achieve these outcomes. The current AI systems are embedded in political and economic systems that were developed generations ago and are not designed to manage widespread computation or automated cognition. As a result, the gaps in these systems and their proclivity to be exploited are influencing how AI is being developed, deployed, and used. It is essential to separate the technological problems of AI from the socio-political problems, as they require different solutions.

To address the socio-political problems of AI, structural reforms are needed, including decoupling the social and technological aspects of AI. This can be achieved by forcing companies to pay the energy and environmental costs of AI development, taxing profits adequately and redistributing them, enforcing antitrust laws, and making corporations responsible to stakeholders beyond their majority shareholders. By addressing the problems with capitalism that AI is exacerbating, we can build sustainable AI that benefits society as a whole, rather than just consolidating power and wealth. Alternative pathways for AI development, such as the Swiss model, demonstrate that it is possible to develop AI in a way that serves the public interest, rather than just private capital gains.

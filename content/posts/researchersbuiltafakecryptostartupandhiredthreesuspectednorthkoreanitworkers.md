Security researchers created a fake cryptocurrency startup, Ballena Azul, to investigate North Korean IT workers' attempts to gain access to companies' internal systems. The researchers advertised developer jobs and hired three individuals they believed to be North Korean operatives. Each hire went through the standard onboarding process, including interviews and contract signing, and was given access to a virtual machine (VM) that was secretly recording their activities.

The researchers found that the hired individuals used fake identities, including driver's licenses and social security numbers, which were likely created using AI-powered image editing software. The images contained metadata and watermarks that suggested they were processed using Google's Gemini app. The operatives also used various tools, including Chrome Remote Desktop, GitHub, and AI-powered job application extensions, to carry out their work. The researchers observed that the operatives' behavior was focused on reconnaissance, including profiling their machines and checking their connection's country of origin.

The researchers' findings highlight the risks of hiring North Korean IT workers, who may be seeking to gain access to companies' internal systems and source code. The researchers advise companies to conduct periodic identity checks, use in-person verification for remote workers, and block certain VPNs, such as AstrillVPN, which has been linked to North Korean operations. The study's results were presented at DEF CON 34, and the researchers believe that the hired individuals are likely part of the Famous Chollima group, a North Korean IT worker operation. However, the attribution has not been confirmed by government sources, and the real names behind the three personas remain unknown.

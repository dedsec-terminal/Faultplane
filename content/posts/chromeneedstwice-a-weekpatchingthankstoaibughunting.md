Google's Chrome browser is experiencing a significant increase in security patches due to the use of AI bug hunting tools. The Chrome security team has reported that the browser's two major version releases in June included fixes for 1,072 security bugs, which is more than the team had shipped in the previous 23 big releases combined. This surge in patches is largely driven by the team's internal process for using AI tools in vulnerability discovery, triage, and patch development.

The Chrome security team is now moving towards a new normal of pushing out a major release every two weeks with additional weekly security updates. However, the team is piloting a cadence of releasing security fixes twice a week due to the intense frenzy of vulnerability discoveries. The team's use of AI models and capabilities has been successful in finding and fixing new bugs, and they are training their models to have an encyclopedic understanding of how software projects have evolved over time.

The Chrome security team is not only focused on patching vulnerabilities but also on making structural changes to the browser's design to prevent whole categories of common bugs. They are rewriting portions of C++ code in the more secure programming language Rust, which is "memory safe". The team believes that the current spike in patches is a temporary inflection point, and that a new equilibrium will be reached as the industry incorporates AI into their development workflows. However, they also acknowledge that making software more secure will require ongoing effort and investment.

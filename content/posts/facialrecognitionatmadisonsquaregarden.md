Madison Square Garden (MSG) has been using facial recognition software to scan everyone entering the facility. The system flags certain individuals, including activists who oppose the use of facial recognition technology. Ironically, one of the flagged individuals, Evan Greer, pointed out that Taylor Swift, who recently had her wedding at MSG, has used facial recognition at her concerts to identify stalkers.

The facial recognition system at MSG was temporarily shut off for Taylor Swift's wedding, highlighting a "privacy for me, surveillance for thee" attitude. This raises concerns about the unequal access to privacy, where wealthy individuals like Swift can afford to protect their privacy, while others are subject to surveillance. Swift's use of non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) for her wedding guests also sparked criticism, with some comparing it to a business expense rather than a personal celebration.

The incident has sparked a debate about the use of facial recognition technology and the erosion of privacy in public spaces. The fact that Swift's wedding was able to maintain privacy, with no photos leaking online, underscores the unequal access to privacy and the ability of the wealthy to control their personal information. The criticism of Swift's use of NDAs and facial recognition technology highlights the need for a more nuanced discussion about the balance between privacy and security in public spaces.

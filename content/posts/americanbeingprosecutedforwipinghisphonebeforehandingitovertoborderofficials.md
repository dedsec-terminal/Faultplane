An American citizen, identified as Tunick, is being prosecuted for wiping his phone before handing it over to border officials. The phone was running GrapheneOS, a custom Android operating system that includes a feature allowing the device owner to set a passcode that deliberately wipes the contents of the device if entered. Tunick's attorneys confirmed that this feature was used, resulting in the phone being wiped before it was handed over to the authorities.

The case raises questions about constitutional rights at the border, which the US government considers not to be US soil until a person is authorized to enter. Tunick was not under arrest at the time, adding to the complexity of the situation. The GrapheneOS feature used by Tunick is considered "completely legal" by its developers, who argue that weakening the security protections would be unconstitutional.

The prosecution of Tunick has sparked concerns about the balance between national security and individual rights. The use of GrapheneOS and its security features is protected by the US Constitution, according to the developers. The outcome of the case will likely have implications for the use of secure operating systems and the rights of individuals at the border. The situation highlights the ongoing debate about the limits of government power and individual freedoms in the digital age.

Here is a summary of the article in 3 concise paragraphs:

New York City lawmakers are pushing to ban the use of facial-recognition technology at Madison Square Garden (MSG) and other public spaces in the city. City Council member Shahana Hanif has proposed a bill, dubbed "Ban the Scan," which would prohibit public venues from using biometric recognition tech. Hanif and other advocates, including musicians and digital rights groups, rallied outside MSG on Friday to raise awareness about the issue and build support for the legislation.

MSG has been criticized for its extensive use of biometric data collection, including tracking the movements of fans and maintaining a database of hundreds of individuals who have been banned from the venue. The database includes information on celebrities and VIPs, as well as the race and sexual orientation of attendees. Hanif and other advocates argue that this level of surveillance is an invasion of privacy and poses a threat to public safety. MSG has defended its use of facial-recognition technology, saying it is simply a modern way of recognizing people.

Hanif's bill has gained significant support, with 27 endorsements from City Council members, more than half of the total council. The bill is currently in talks with Speaker Julie Menin to advance the legislation. Hanif, who grew up in post-9/11 New York, says she is particularly concerned about the level of surveillance at MSG because of its potential to be used for discriminatory purposes. She argues that New Yorkers need to be aware of the extent of surveillance they are exposed to and take action to protect their privacy and safety.

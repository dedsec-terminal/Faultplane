At the Black Hat security conference, researcher James Kettle presented his findings on the capabilities of agentic AI in developing novel hacking methods. Kettle's research aimed to explore whether AI can devise new attack paths in a fully autonomous way, and his conclusion was that AI is minimally capable but extremely limited in this regard. However, when paired with human guidance and insight, AI becomes a powerful partner in conceptualizing and uncovering new strategies for hacking.

Kettle's research involved using AI models to analyze web security vulnerabilities, and he discovered a new area of potential vulnerability called Shared-Parser Confusion. This finding was significant because it highlights a major attack surface that could potentially lead to various types of attacks. Kettle's discovery was the result of a human-AI collaboration, where the AI system analyzed data and came up with a hypothesis that Kettle then evaluated and confirmed. This collaboration demonstrates the potential of AI to contribute to cybersecurity work, both in defensive and offensive hacking.

Kettle's experiments involved using AI models to analyze web security data and identify potential vulnerabilities. He found that the AI systems were able to generate notable findings at a rapid pace, creating a productive research feedback loop. While the AI system was not able to prove the Shared-Parser Confusion vulnerability on its own, it was able to analyze data and come up with a hypothesis that Kettle then confirmed. This collaboration highlights the importance of human-AI collaboration in cybersecurity research, where AI can generate leads and hypotheses, and humans can evaluate and confirm them, leading to significant discoveries and advancements in the field.
